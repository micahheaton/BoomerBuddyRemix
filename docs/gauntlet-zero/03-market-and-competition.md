# Market and Competition

Status: **category need is proven; BoomerBuddy demand and differentiation are not**. Research accessed 2026-08-15.

## Market reality

**Facts.** Americans age 60+ reported $2.4 billion in fraud losses in 2024, four times the 2020 figure; the FTC estimates actual loss was much higher but gives a wide $10.1–$81.5 billion range because underreporting is uncertain ([FTC, December 2025](https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/ftc_gov/pdf/P144400-OlderAdultsReportDec2025.pdf)). The U.S. had 61.2 million people age 65+ in 2024 ([Census, June 2025](https://www.census.gov/newsroom/press-releases/2025/older-adults-outnumber-children.html)). The 2025 AARP/NAC study estimates 63 million family caregivers, including 59 million caring for adults ([AARP, July 2025](https://www.aarp.org/press/releases/2025-07-24-new-report-reveals-crisis-point-for-americas-63-million-family-caregivers.html)).

These figures prove a large problem, not a serviceable market. “65+,” caregivers, and credit-union memberships overlap heavily and must never be added into a TAM. Reported or suspicious dollar volume is not vendor revenue.

**Initial beachhead hypothesis.** The first payer is an adult child or caregiver who worries about a parent living independently, already receives “is this real?” messages, and wants a calm shared response. The protected adult remains an equal user with revocable consent. Validate this against direct older-adult purchase and institution-sponsored use.

## Defensible position to test

BoomerBuddy should compete on **time from uncertainty to a safe, verified action**, not classification. A potentially valuable bundle is:

- senior-first capture and explanations across message, image, call and URL artifacts;
- evidence separated from inference, with honest uncertainty;
- consented family investigation, discussion and handoff;
- verified contact/recovery paths and follow-through;
- measured accessibility, safety quality and household retention.

This is a hypothesis, not a moat. Models, reputation data and UI can be copied. Defensibility would have to accumulate through trusted distribution, a consented collaboration graph, properly licensed labeled incident/evaluation data, proven workflows and recurring use.

## Validation and kill tests

1. Interview 15 older adults and 15 adult children using recent real incidents. Record current workaround, urgency, who asks whom, desired action and payer; do not ask leading willingness-to-pay questions.
2. Run a blinded task test against a general AI assistant and one purpose-built competitor on at least 30 representative artifacts. Measure safe-action completion, harmful advice, time, comprehension and confidence calibration—not just labels.
3. Offer a transparent concierge beta to 50 households for 90 days. Measure second incident use, Trusted Circle activation, safe-action completion, support burden and paid conversion at disclosed prices.
4. Conduct 12 institutional discovery interviews, including at least six credit unions, without presenting a “partnership” as secured.

**Stop or reposition** if users value only a one-time verdict; repeat household use is absent; free tools perform the full job; consented family participation is low; or support and intelligence costs prevent sustainable margin.
