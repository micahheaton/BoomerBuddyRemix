# Partner and Channel Strategy

Status: **sequencing proposal; no partner interest, economics or performance is established**. Research accessed 2026-08-15.

## Channel design

Use partners for one of four explicit jobs: **audience access, sponsored eligibility, trusted workflow placement, or embedded distribution**. A logo, content swap or introduction is not a channel. Each relationship needs a named audience, consent-safe journey, economic owner, attribution method, support model and renewal event.

## Priority by evidence required

| Channel | Entry offer | Why now / why later | Proof required |
|---|---|---|---|
| Credit unions | Paid member-benefit evaluation | Strong mission/trust fit, reachable official universe; direct incumbent and diligence burden | Enrollment, repeat household value, acceptable support, renewal |
| Caregiver/retirement media, newsletters and affinity groups | Sourced content, webinar or disclosed affiliate | Fast message/audience learning; weak moat and endorsement risk | Qualified activated households and contribution after share |
| RIAs and broker-dealers | Client/family education plus low-integration benefit | Trusted relationships and family-risk conversations; compliance review | Adviser adoption, client use, reusable workflow |
| Employer caregiver benefits and brokers | Sponsored caregiver cohort | AARP/NAC estimates 63 million caregivers and widespread work disruption ([AARP, 2025](https://www.aarp.org/press/releases/2025-07-24-new-report-reveals-crisis-point-for-americas-63-million-family-caregivers.html)); crowded benefit stack | Eligible-to-activated, employee satisfaction, annual renewal |
| Senior living, home care and elder-law/estate professionals | Family orientation or referral | High-context moment; fragmented budgets and staff workflow | Ethical consent, staff burden, qualified adoption |
| Insurers | Defined prevention/engagement evaluation | Potential claims value; long cycle and difficult loss attribution | Longitudinal outcome evidence and actuarial sponsor |
| Telecom, identity/security and OEM | Bundle, API or acquisition discussion | Large distribution; strong build-versus-buy leverage and integration | Proven demand, technical differentiation, scale and rights |
| Libraries/community organizations | Education and access, grant-supported where real | Trusted inclusion and learning; limited direct budget | Reach to underserved users without unsustainable support |

[FINRA’s trusted-contact guidance](https://www.finra.org/investors/insights/senior-financial-exploitation) makes advisers/broker-dealers a relevant discovery segment, but a trusted contact cannot transact and BoomerBuddy must not blur that boundary. [Aura’s 2025 Life360 partnership](https://www.aura.com/press/release/aura-and-life360-announce-strategic-partnership) also shows that family-security vendors pursue bundled distribution; it is vendor evidence of competition, not proof this channel works for BoomerBuddy.

## Sequence

1. **Learn:** direct B2C, community education and narrowly tracked publisher tests establish message, activation and support cost.
2. **Prove sponsored distribution:** run a reusable credit-union design-partner offer; explore RIA and employer-benefit buyers in parallel discovery only.
3. **Repeat:** choose one sponsored vertical after renewal evidence and standardize sales, security, launch and reporting.
4. **Expand:** approach insurers or embedded/OEM partners only with retained cohorts, defensible quality and contract/data readiness.

Pursuing every named channel at once would create custom work, conflicting roadmaps and false pipeline. The founder should set one primary revenue channel and one learning channel per quarter.

## Commercial forms

- **Referral:** partner introduces users; BoomerBuddy owns customer, billing and support.
- **Affiliate:** tracked introduction with clearly disclosed compensation; pay after a qualified, non-refunded purchase.
- **Sponsor:** partner funds access for an eligible group and receives only contracted aggregate reporting.
- **Reseller:** partner owns commercial relationship; requires support, claims, data, pricing and renewal controls.
- **Embedded/API:** product lives in partner workflow; highest integration, security, service-level and concentration risk.

Do not white-label early if hiding BoomerBuddy prevents direct trust, learning and brand equity. Do not give exclusivity without a minimum, defined scope/term, performance obligation and termination right.

## Partner scorecard

Before committing, score documented evidence for:

- audience-job fit and reachable eligible volume;
- trusted access and ability to communicate repeatedly;
- economic buyer, budget, timing and existing alternative;
- activation ownership and implementation effort;
- data sensitivity, regulatory/security review and reputational risk;
- expected net revenue after commission, launch, support and sales cost;
- reusability, concentration, reference rights and contract assignability.

Keep “interest,” qualified opportunity, signed evaluation, launched cohort and renewed production account as separate stages. Probability-weighted pipeline must be based on observed stage conversion, not founder optimism.

## Operating and claims controls

Every agreement should cover brand/claim approval, endorsement disclosure, data roles, consent, security incidents, accessibility, support escalation, service levels, intellectual property, fees, audit, term/renewal, termination, customer transition and assignment. The [FTC Endorsement Guides FAQ](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/ftcs-endorsement-guides-what-people-are-asking) requires clear material-connection disclosure for compensated recommendations.

Never call an organization a customer, partner, adviser, design partner or endorser until the corresponding written permission exists. Partner-submitted user artifacts remain untrusted and may not be reused for training, case studies or marketing without an explicit lawful basis and rights.

## Validation plan

Run 30 structured buyer interviews across the top four rows, then no more than three small audience/referral experiments and two paid sponsored evaluations. For each, pre-register eligible reach, activation, safe-action completion, 90-day survival, complaints, support, net contribution and renewal decision. Graduate a channel only after repeatable activation and one renewal/expansion; exit when net economics depend on hidden custom labor, misleading claims, sensitive-data access before value or one partner dominates the business.
