# Human Research Moderator Guides

Status: **scripted guides awaiting approved external execution; no sessions or findings**

Protocol: [Human Research Protocol](./HUMAN-RESEARCH-PROTOCOL.md) `run2-human-research-v1`

## Moderator rules

- Read bold disclosure text verbatim. Use neutral prompts only; never sell, reassure, teach the answer, or imply the prototype succeeded.
- Use participant IDs, synthetic examples, and the approved research environment. Do not request real suspicious messages, accounts, contact details, secrets, or incident narratives.
- Record observation before interpretation. If assistance is needed, code it at the moment it occurs.
- Do not let one member of a pair answer consent, privacy, or comprehension questions for the other.
- Follow every stop rule in the protocol. Research is not a support or fraud-response channel.

## Common opening script

> **Thank you for helping us evaluate an experimental product. This is research, not a safety, financial, legal, medical, or emergency service. BoomerBuddy can be wrong and does not guarantee that something is safe. Please use only the examples we provide today—do not paste or describe a real suspicious message, link, account, code, or private situation. You may skip any question, ask for an accommodation or break, or stop at any time without losing the incentive you have earned.**

Confirm aloud and record separately:

1. “Do you agree to participate in this research session?”
2. “Do you agree to the data handling described in the information sheet?”
3. If recording is approved: “Do you separately agree to recording?” A `no` continues without recording.
4. If quotation is proposed: “May we use deidentified quotes internally?” A `no` has no effect on participation.

Ask: “Before we begin, what would you like adjusted for vision, hearing, movement, reading, language, pace, or comfort?” Apply the approved accommodation and record only its category.

## Older-adult usability guide — 75 minutes

### 1. Context interview — 10 minutes

- “When something online feels suspicious, what do you usually do first?”
- “Who, if anyone, do you ask for help?”
- “What makes a safety tool feel respectful or disrespectful?”
- “What would make you stop using one?”

Do not probe for a real incident. If one is volunteered, redirect: “Thank you. To protect your privacy, I’m not going to ask for details. We’ll use the supplied example.”

### 2. Public Check task — 15 minutes

Prompt: “Imagine this supplied fictional message appeared on your phone. Starting from this page, find what you would do next.”

Observe without instruction: landing comprehension, input choice, result comprehension, whether the participant attempts to open a link, safe-action selection, false-safety language, time, and assistance. Then ask:

- “In your own words, what does BoomerBuddy know here?”
- “What does it not know?”
- “What would you do next, and why?”
- “Does this result prove the message is safe or fraudulent?”

### 3. Save/account boundary — 10 minutes

Prompt: “You may continue without saving or choose the optional save path. Show me what you think each choice does.”

Ask:

- “What information becomes connected to an account?”
- “Who can see it?”
- “Does a campaign, referrer, payer, or household administrator get access?”
- “How would you decide whether to save?”

Do not complete registration or send an invitation.

### 4. Orientation and Family task — 25 minutes

Use the synthetic household. Prompt: “Complete setup as the protected person. Tell me what you expect before selecting each step.”

Observe protected-person confirmation, Trusted Circle explanation, safe-word choice/deferral, practice Check behavior, capability-limit comprehension, and completion. Ask after the task:

- “What can a Trusted Circle person see?”
- “What can the person paying see just because they pay?”
- “Who can stop sharing?”
- “What happens to other relationships if one relationship ends?”
- “Is the safe word identity proof? What should happen if it is forgotten?”
- “What help or notification did you expect but could not find?”

### 5. Debrief — 15 minutes

- “What felt clear? What felt uncomfortable or patronizing?”
- “Where did you expect human help?”
- “What single change would most improve trust?”
- Open response first, then record 1–5 comprehension, confidence, and trust ratings.
- Read the common closing script.

## Adult-child individual guide — 30 minutes

- “What job are you trying to do when someone asks whether a message is real?”
- “What should remain controlled by the older adult even if you pay?”
- “What would feel like surveillance?”
- “Which failures would make you cancel or warn others?”
- Show the authority diagram. Ask the participant to explain administrator, payer/billing, protected person, Trusted Circle, and content permission without moderator correction.
- Present the randomized plan cards. Ask which plan, if any, fits; what is missing; what is unnecessary; and why. Record rejection as valid evidence.
- Ask four price boundaries for the selected plan: “so inexpensive you would question it,” “good value,” “starting to feel expensive,” and “too expensive to consider.” Record exact concept/version and rationale. Do not call the response willingness-to-pay proof.

## Protected-person individual guide — 30 minutes

- “What help would you want, and what would you never want another person to see or control?”
- “If someone else pays, what do you think they should learn?”
- “How would you want an invitation and consent request explained?”
- “What would make it easy or hard to say no?”
- “How should withdrawal work?”
- Show the same randomized brand/positioning cards independently. Do not reveal the adult-child answers.

Before the joint session ask privately: “Do you still want to participate together, and are there any topics or screens we should not show jointly?” A `no` ends the paired portion without penalty.

## Paired Family task guide — 60 minutes

1. Reconfirm consent with each person separately.
2. Assign the protected-person and purchaser roles using the synthetic household; do not let payment imply access.
3. Prompt: “Together, set up the relationship you both want. Say what you believe each action permits.”
4. Ask the protected person to accept, narrow, or decline. The purchaser must not operate their controls.
5. Use a synthetic shared Check and a separate unshared Check. Ask each person to predict visibility before viewing.
6. Exercise withdrawal of one relationship. Ask what unrelated membership, payment, and sharing remain.
7. Ask each participant separately to explain: “Who can see what, who can change payment, who can withdraw, and what BoomerBuddy cannot know?”

Stop on coercion, disagreement about recording/sharing, hidden-monitoring expectation, or a real artifact. Record who initiated decisions and every moderator prompt.

## Brand, positioning, and pricing module — 20 minutes

Run after product tasks to reduce priming. Use a pre-generated balanced order and opaque card IDs.

For each card:

- “What do you think this is?”
- “Who is it for?”
- “What promise does it make?”
- “Which words increase or reduce trust?”
- “Does anything sound patronizing, frightening, deceptive, or like a guarantee?”

After all cards:

- Rank for clarity and trust; explain the difference.
- Ask unaided recall of the working name and core job.
- Show Free, Plus, and Family cards in randomized order. Ask the participant to sort features, identify who pays/benefits, and choose/reject with rationale.
- Ask the four price boundaries and what evidence, service, cancellation, or support would be required.

Do not report preference as conversion, retention, or trademark clearance. Do not introduce a renamed public brand under this protocol.

## Credit-union buyer guide — 45 minutes

Opening addition:

> **This is discovery, not a sales proposal. We will not ask for member data, confidential controls, a commitment, or permission to contact members. Institution and role facts will remain research evidence unless separately authorized and verified.**

### Current process — 15 minutes

- “How does a member fraud concern enter your organization today?”
- “Who owns prevention education, incident response, digital product, and member support?”
- “Where do cases stall, duplicate effort, or create member harm?”
- “Which metrics are trusted, and which claims would your institution reject?”

### Concept and data boundary — 15 minutes

Show the institutional concept and aggregate-reporting boundary without price first.

- “Which problem, if any, is this solving?”
- “What data must never leave the member or BoomerBuddy?”
- “Would eligibility, activation, and paid/revenue-bearing status be reported separately?”
- “What small-cell, consent, security, accessibility, support, and incident evidence is required?”
- “Which systems or teams would own integration?”

### Approval and economics — 10 minutes

- “Who would own evaluation, budget, compliance, implementation, renewal, and termination?”
- “What is the smallest meaningful pilot outcome?”
- “What would stop a pilot before launch?”
- “What contract, DPA, insurance, accessibility, security, or audit evidence is mandatory?”
- “Which pricing unit would be understandable: eligible member, activated household, paid household, or fixed program—and why?”

### Close — 5 minutes

- “What did we misunderstand?”
- “Who else would need independent research, without implying an introduction?”
- “May we record that your role identified this workflow requirement?”

Do not change an HQ opportunity stage, create forecast revenue, or infer intent from the interview without the separate evidence policy.

## Neutral prompt ladder

Use in order and record each use:

1. Silence for at least five seconds.
2. “What are you thinking?”
3. “What would you expect to happen?”
4. “Please reread the task in your own words.”
5. “Where would you look next?”
6. Environment recovery only, without explaining product meaning.

Any product-specific direction makes completion assisted. Safety intervention ends the task and is always critical evidence.

## Common closing script

> **That completes the research task. This prototype did not evaluate a real message and today’s session does not mean the product is ready or that any plan, partnership, or price is approved. Please do not rely on it for a current incident. We will handle and delete research data according to the information sheet. You may contact the named research owner with questions or a withdrawal request.**

Confirm incentive logistics without adding the participant to marketing, product accounts, CRM pipeline, or future contact lists unless separately and explicitly consented.
