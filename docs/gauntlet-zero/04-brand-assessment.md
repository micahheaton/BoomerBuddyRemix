# Brand Assessment

Status: **usable working name, not cleared or validated**. Research accessed 2026-08-15.

## Verdict

“BoomerBuddy” is memorable, friendly and immediately suggests an older-adult audience. It also risks sounding age-coded, patronizing, temporary and narrower than the actual buying household. Treat it as a provisional product name until audience, domain and legal work is complete.

## Evidence and risks

- **Audience fit.** Baby boomers were born from 1946 through 1964 and are roughly 62–80 in 2026 ([Pew Research Center, January 2026](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2026/01/09/the-oldest-baby-boomers-turn-80-in-2026/)). The label excludes many older Silent Generation users and will age poorly as Gen X enters the use case.
- **Dignity.** The National Institute on Aging explicitly lists “boomer” among terms to avoid when they carry negative connotations ([NIA, December 2023](https://www.nia.nih.gov/research/blog/2023/12/dont-call-me-old-avoiding-ageism-when-writing-about-aging)). AARP’s language research found that context and associations influence how adults 50+ respond ([AARP](https://www.aarp.org/pri/topics/aging-experience/language-aging/)). Neither source proves this name will fail; both make direct testing mandatory.
- **Digital confusion.** As of the access date, [boomerbuddy.net](https://boomerbuddy.net/) was live, while [boomerbuddy.ca](https://boomerbuddy.ca/) and [boomer-buddies.com](https://boomer-buddies.com/) used similar wording for unrelated offerings. The v1 code also uses “Boomer Buddy” and .com-style references. This creates recall, phishing and support friction even without a legal conflict.
- **Legal clearance.** No trademark clearance is documented. The USPTO warns that a comprehensive search covers confusingly similar marks, related goods/services and sources beyond its federal database ([USPTO search guidance](https://www.uspto.gov/trademarks/search/federal-trademark-searching)). A web or exact-name search is not clearance.
- **Claims trust.** The v1 About page states that a founder’s family member lost $10,000 and says the service is “trusted by families” (reference/boomerbuddy-v1/BoomerBuddy/client/src/pages/About.tsx). No substantiation was reviewed. These claims must be removed unless the founder can document and approve them; testimonials must be genuine, permissioned and representative.

## Brand system

The brand promise should be: **“From suspicious to a safer next step—together.”** Lead with calm, agency and verification. Avoid “elderly,” “vulnerable,” “foolproof,” “always protected,” “AI knows,” fear-based loss counters and childish mascots. Refer to “you,” “your family,” “people you trust,” and “older adults” only when demographic precision matters.

Required trust signals:

- identify the operating company, support channels, privacy choices and subscription terms plainly;
- distinguish evidence, model inference and unknowns;
- never imply bank, government, law-enforcement, AARP or credit-union endorsement without a signed, current agreement;
- use only verified statistics with year, population and source;
- make sponsorship, affiliate compensation and incentivized endorsements conspicuous.

## Decision options

1. **Keep BoomerBuddy as a product name** if both users and caregivers find it respectful and recall the correct domain.
2. **Use a broader company/master brand with “BoomerBuddy” as a feature or campaign** if institutional buyers like the clarity but users dislike the label.
3. **Rename before material spend** if testing shows embarrassment, mistaken targeting, weak verbal recall or material clearance risk.

Do not commission a large identity system, paid media or printed partner materials before this gate.

## Validation plan

Run moderated interviews with 12 adults age 65+, 12 adult children/caregivers and six prospective institutional buyers. Compare BoomerBuddy against three neutral candidates in randomized order. Test unaided interpretation, respect, trust, relevance, spelling, domain recall and willingness to recommend; do not ask “Do you like it?” alone. Include screen-reader and telephone comprehension.

Then ask trademark counsel to conduct federal, state, common-law, domain and social-handle clearance for the finalists. Separately document ownership of domains and handles. The founder makes the name decision only after reviewing segmented results; a simple majority can conceal a severe trust problem in the protected-adult segment.
