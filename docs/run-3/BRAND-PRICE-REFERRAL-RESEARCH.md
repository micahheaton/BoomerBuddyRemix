# Brand, Price, and Referral Research

Status: **research design only; no participant, conversion, price, or brand evidence exists**

Last reviewed: 2026-08-16

This package turns the Run 3 commercial hypotheses into falsifiable human-research questions. It does not authorize recruitment, marketing, recording, referral outreach, a price change, or a testimonial. Synthetic critique may improve the materials, but it never counts as participant evidence.

The governed study procedures remain the [Human Research Protocol](../run-2/HUMAN-RESEARCH-PROTOCOL.md), [Moderator Guides](../run-2/HUMAN-RESEARCH-MODERATOR-GUIDES.md), and [Research Forms](../run-2/HUMAN-RESEARCH-FORMS.md). This document narrows their Run 3 commercial module.

## Questions to answer

1. Does “BoomerBuddy” feel memorable and approachable, or patronizing and age-coded?
2. Does “Buddy” communicate useful support without implying expertise, monitoring, or protection the product cannot provide?
3. Do older adults and adult children independently understand who uses, benefits from, administers, and pays for the service?
4. Which position is clearest and most credible: “scam detector,” “family scam-safety network,” or “safer-action service”?
5. Do participants understand the uncertainty, evidence, privacy, Trusted Circle, and emergency boundaries before seeing a price?
6. How do the current Free, Plus, and Family hypotheses compare on comprehension and tradeoffs?
7. Does the current first-dollar Family monthly hypothesis of `$14.99` feel plausible after the exact feature and support boundary is shown? This is a test stimulus, not a validated or permanent price.
8. Does annual billing create unacceptable commitment or refund anxiety? What cancellation, support, or proof would reduce that concern?
9. Is “Founding Household” understood as a time-bounded beta benefit rather than “free forever,” preferential treatment, or proof of product maturity?
10. Can a referral-credit concept be explained without transferred consent, automatic contact messaging, guaranteed savings, or coercive family pressure?

## Hypotheses under attack

| ID | Hypothesis | Strong disconfirming evidence | Decision consequence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| B1 | BoomerBuddy is friendly and memorable | Repeated unprompted patronization, age stereotyping, embarrassment, or refusal to share the name across distinct cohorts | Pause brand commitment; test neutral candidates before public launch |
| B2 | “Family scam-safety network” explains the product | Participants infer surveillance, shared inbox access, automatic protection, or guaranteed detection | Prefer a narrower safer-action position and revise orientation |
| B3 | The adult child may buy while the older adult remains the protected decision-maker | Either party assumes payer or administrator authority overrides the protected adult's consent | Block buyer-positioned acquisition copy until authority comprehension improves |
| P1 | `$14.99/month` is a plausible Family first-dollar stimulus | Value/expensive boundaries consistently fall below it after comprehension, or support/refund expectations make the offer structurally uneconomic | Do not activate the price; revise offer or service boundary |
| P2 | Annual billing can be considered after monthly proof | Commitment, cancellation, refund, or trust objections dominate the benefit | Keep annual unavailable for first dollar |
| F1 | “Founding Household” communicates bounded beta access | Participants hear lifetime benefit, testimonial obligation, exclusivity pressure, or production readiness | Rename/rewrite and withhold cohort enrollment |
| R1 | User-initiated sharing is acceptable | Participants believe BoomerBuddy receives contacts, sends the first message, or rewards mere phone/email entry | Keep referral credit disabled and redesign disclosure/mechanic |
| R2 | Recipient-qualified milestones are comprehensible | Referrers expect credit before recipient acceptance/eligibility or cannot explain clawback/caps | Do not publish terms or enable credits |

No hypothesis passes from a single favorable quote or synthetic persona.

## Concepts to compare

Use accessible, content-equivalent concept cards. Randomize order and record it.

### Working brand card

- Name: **BoomerBuddy**.
- Plain claim: “A family scam-safety service that helps people examine suspicious messages, understand uncertainty, and choose a safer next step.”
- Boundary: it is not emergency response, law enforcement, financial advice, monitoring, or a guarantee that a message is safe.

### Positioning cards

1. **Scam detector** — deliberately included as an aggressive claim to test misunderstanding and rejection; it must not appear as approved copy.
2. **Family scam-safety network** — emphasizes consented family support.
3. **Safer-action service** — emphasizes decision support and uncertainty.

Every card must show the same privacy, evidence, and no-guarantee boundary so wording is the tested variable.

### Brand-name alternatives

Test the working name against two or more neutral placeholders selected before sessions. Candidate names must pass trademark/domain/professional review before adoption; participant preference alone cannot clear those gates. Do not expose a real unregistered domain or imply that a candidate is available.

### Price and offer cards

- Show features and limitations before price.
- Show Free, Plus, and Family in randomized order using the frozen concept version.
- Include the current repository hypotheses (`$8.99/month` Plus and `$14.99/month` Family) only as research stimuli.
- Treat annual prices, discounts, trials, promotions, tax, referral credits, and Founding Household benefits as separate variables rather than bundling them.
- Ask the four price-boundary questions: suspiciously inexpensive, good value, expensive but possible, and too expensive.
- Record what evidence, support, cancellation, refund, and privacy guarantees participants require.

Stated choice is not conversion, willingness-to-pay proof, price elasticity, CAC, churn, or revenue.

### Referral cards

Compare at least:

- no financial credit, user-initiated share only;
- the Run 3 starting hypothesis: one month-equivalent candidate credit after a qualified recipient action and up to three months total after an eligible annual paid conversion;
- a bounded `1 + 3` alternative;
- a capped Founding Household benefit; and
- no referral program.

Each card must state that entering a contact earns nothing; BoomerBuddy does not upload contacts or send the first invitation; the recipient acts and consents independently; self-referral/duplicates/refunds/disputes may invalidate or reverse credit; caps and expiration apply; and final tax/accounting treatment remains under professional review.

## Cohorts and sampling

Follow the minimums and recruitment controls in the Run 2 protocol. The Run 3 module must include, without pretending they are representative of the market:

- older adults with varied digital confidence;
- adult children or caregivers who could plausibly purchase;
- consented paired families, interviewed separately before joint work;
- users of screen readers, magnification, voice control, switch/keyboard access, or other assistive technology;
- fraud/cyber or consumer-protection professionals; and
- financial-institution stakeholders for institutional positioning only.

Recruitment and research consent stay separate from marketing, a Founding Household invitation, sales follow-up, and testimonial permission. Do not recruit from purchased or scraped contact data. Compensating research participants is not product demand or referral conversion.

If those participants are unavailable, record the corresponding row as `pending_human`; do not fill it with synthetic results.

## Session sequence

1. Confirm research consent, recording choice, accessibility needs, retention purpose, withdrawal route, and emergency boundary.
2. Capture unprompted expectations from the name alone.
3. Show product workflow and authority/privacy boundary without pricing.
4. Run comprehension-back questions before revealing alternate positioning.
5. Randomize and compare positioning/name concepts.
6. Complete one synthetic task; do not use a participant's active incident.
7. Show feature cards, then price stimuli, then annual/Founding Household variants.
8. Show referral concepts only after confirming user-initiated sharing and recipient consent.
9. Separate participant preference, comprehension failure, safety concern, feature request, pricing objection, and purchase-language response.
10. Debrief that no concept, price, referral offer, or product state is approved and the session does not create service or marketing consent.

Moderators must not defend “Boomer,” coach participants into the Trusted Circle answer, negotiate price, sell access, or ask whether the participant would “definitely buy.”

## Evidence record

For each observation, retain only the approved minimum:

- participant cohort and accessibility category, pseudonymized;
- consent/research-purpose/version and recording choice;
- concept and order versions;
- task outcome and moderator assistance;
- comprehension-back result;
- structured trust/patronization/authority signals;
- exact price-boundary responses and rationale when consented;
- referral/Founding Household interpretation;
- adverse-event, withdrawal, or safety flag;
- analyst classification and independent reviewer disposition; and
- evidence tier, source artifact digest, retention deadline, and deletion/restriction state.

Do not place raw Check content, contact information, credentials, OTPs, card data, safe words, or unnecessary narratives in analytics or the research summary. Quotes require separate quote-specific consent; publication or testimonial use requires a later, artifact-specific permission and review.

## Scorecard and decision rules

Report denominators and missing data for every cohort. Use a predeclared scorecard:

- unaided product-purpose comprehension;
- uncertainty/no-guarantee comprehension;
- protected-person, administrator, payer, and Trusted Circle comprehension;
- perceived patronization or age coding;
- perceived usefulness and trust requirements;
- plan/feature comprehension;
- price-boundary distributions, never a single average forecast;
- Founding Household boundary comprehension;
- referral consent/reward/clawback comprehension;
- accessibility task completion and severe blockers; and
- moderator rescue, withdrawals, adverse events, and unresolved disagreements.

Before the study begins, the founder and research owner must set the minimum sample, segment balance, comprehension threshold, severe-blocker rule, disagreement process, and decision owner. Changing thresholds after seeing results must be recorded as a protocol amendment, not hidden.

Decision vocabulary:

- `pending_human` — no qualifying real participant evidence;
- `insufficient` — evidence exists but sample, quality, accessibility, or disagreement prevents a decision;
- `revise_and_retest` — a defined concept or flow failed its predeclared rule;
- `candidate_supported` — bounded evidence supports another test, not launch or market truth;
- `blocked_safety_or_ethics` — patronization, coercion, privacy, consent, accessibility, or participant-harm issue blocks use.

No research disposition enables live Checkout, referral credits, marketing messages, public claims, or app-store submission.

## Independent review

Before a founder decision, separate reviewers must inspect:

- protocol deviations and moderator leading;
- adverse or contradictory evidence;
- exclusion and missing-data decisions;
- accessibility representation;
- whether favorable quotes are over-weighted;
- research/marketing/testimonial consent separation;
- whether price language is being misreported as purchase behavior; and
- whether referral framing creates coercion, transferred consent, or misleading reward expectations.

The skeptical review and founder disposition are append-only evidence. Raw participant data is not copied into Founder Attention.
