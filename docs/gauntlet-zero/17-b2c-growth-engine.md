# B2C Growth Engine

Status: **channel thesis only; no acquisition or retention channel is proven**. Research accessed 2026-08-15.

## Growth thesis

Lead with the moment a real message, call or request creates uncertainty. The first growth loop is:

**current scam question → useful free check → safe action → optional household account → consented Trusted Circle → repeat household value → paid plan → user-initiated recommendation**

This avoids asking a cold visitor to buy abstract “protection.” The primary acquisition hypothesis is an adult child/caregiver searching on behalf of a parent; direct older-adult acquisition must be tested separately, not treated as a secondary persona.

## Lifecycle and events

| Stage | User evidence | Required event |
|---|---|---|
| Visitor | Arrives with relevant intent | Qualified landing session and source |
| First value | Receives understandable evidence and next step | Check completed; action viewed |
| Account | Chooses to retain progress | Verified account created |
| Activated household | Returns or coordinates with consent | Second check within 30 days **or** accepted Trusted Circle invitation plus completed action |
| Paid | Purchases knowingly | Settled payment after refund window |
| Retained | Continues to find value | Cohort survival plus meaningful activity |
| Advocate | Recommends voluntarily | Accepted qualified referral, not share click |

Measure each transition by acquisition source, payer persona, artifact type and plan. “Account created” is not activation; an alert opened is not harm prevented.

## Channel portfolio

**Test first**

- **High-intent search and original scam pages.** Create pages around current named scams and “is this message/call real?” jobs, each with a free check and sourced next actions. Google prohibits scaled, unoriginal content made to manipulate rankings ([spam policies](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies)); every page needs human review, original utility, date and sources.
- **YouTube and Facebook education.** In Pew’s 2025 survey, 64% of adults 65+ used YouTube and 57% used Facebook; among adults 50–64, the figures were 85% and 74% ([Pew](https://www.pewresearch.org/internet/fact-sheet/social-media/?level=0)). This supports testing those venues, not assumed performance. Demonstrate a safe action in short, captioned, non-alarmist creative.
- **Earned/newsletter distribution.** Provide timely sourced explainers to local news, caregiver, retirement and community newsletters. Track links and avoid implying editorial endorsement.
- **User-initiated household sharing.** Let a user share a minimal result or invite a trusted person after value. Referral mechanics are defined in [18-referral-engine.md](18-referral-engine.md).

**Test after message/retention proof**

- paid search on narrow urgent-intent terms;
- Facebook/YouTube paid creative segmented by payer;
- affiliates and creators with disclosed compensation;
- caregiver, senior-center, library and professional communities.

Do not scale broad paid social, programmatic display or purchased lead lists before 90-day retained contribution is measurable.

## Experiment system

Run one message × audience × channel cell per campaign. For every experiment record the hypothesis, creative, audience definition, spend, consent copy, landing version, attribution window and stopping rule.

Initial experiments:

1. **Search intent:** three manually researched scam pages, each with the same free-check CTA. Require at least 200 qualified visits/page before comparing check completion and activation.
2. **Payer message:** randomly show “help your parent decide safely” versus “a second opinion for suspicious messages.” Measure comprehension, completion and consent concerns; do not optimize click-through alone.
3. **Video demonstration:** six captioned clips across YouTube and Facebook, split between real workflow and education. Compare qualified checks per dollar and 30-day activation.
4. **Retargeting:** only to consented visitors; cap frequency and suppress anyone in an urgent recovery flow.
5. **Paid conversion:** after meaningful free use, test Plus versus Family framing. Measure paid conversion, refund, support and 90-day survival.

No campaign scales until it has at least 30 settled paid customers, a measured 90-day cohort, known refunds/support cost and an acquisition cost compatible with the contribution-payback gate in [36-unit-economics.md](36-unit-economics.md).

## Attribution and safety controls

Use first-touch, last-non-direct and experiment assignment rather than claiming a single “true” source. Preserve UTM/referral identifiers server-side through signup and payment, deduplicate identities, and report view-through separately. Never upload a contact list or sensitive scam artifact to an ad platform. Collect only the minimum consented analytics and provide opt-out.

Creative must not impersonate banks, government or family emergencies; use fabricated, visibly labeled examples. Never claim guaranteed detection, “loss prevented,” user counts or endorsements without evidence. Paid or incentivized endorsements require clear material-connection disclosure under the [FTC Endorsement Guides](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/ftcs-endorsement-guides-what-people-are-asking).

## 90-day validation outcome

The deliverable is not traffic. It is a source-by-cohort table showing qualified cost per first check, activation, paid conversion, refund, support minutes, 30/60/90-day survival and contribution payback. Pause any source with misleading traffic, disproportionate consent complaints, poor safe-action completion or no credible path to payback.
