# Founder Decision Register

Last updated: 2026-08-16 after Build Run 1. The deeper decision analysis remains in [Gauntlet Zero — Founder Decisions](./gauntlet-zero/42-founder-decisions.md).

Build Run 1 used reversible local hypotheses, so none of these decisions blocked it. They do govern research, spending, or external commitments; the implementation does not decide them by default.

## 1. Brand commitment

- **Issue:** Whether `BoomerBuddy` earns trust with protected older adults, family payers, and institutional sponsors.
- **Recommendation:** Keep it as the working name only; run paired audience/name testing and preliminary trademark review before public commitment.
- **Alternatives:** Commit now, adopt a more neutral trust/safety name, or use different consumer and partner brands.
- **Tradeoff:** Early commitment saves rework but risks patronizing positioning and legal/partner friction; delay preserves options but limits polished brand investment.
- **Why founder / can work continue?:** Brand identity is a durable company choice. Internal research and engineering may continue; public launch and major brand spend should not.

## 2. Packaging and launch price

- **Issue:** No BoomerBuddy willingness-to-pay, retention, support-cost, or channel evidence exists.
- **Recommendation:** Research a useful Free offer, Plus at `$8.99/month` or `$89/year`, and Family at `$14.99/month` or `$149/year`. Reserve `$119/year` for a controlled founding-offer test, not list-price truth.
- **Alternatives:** Family-only, one paid tier, usage-based pricing, or sponsor-funded access.
- **Tradeoff:** More tiers reveal segmentation but add explanation and commerce complexity; a lower annual price may improve conversion while damaging contribution margin, especially through app stores.
- **Why founder / can work continue?:** Pricing encodes positioning and risk appetite. Tests and local entitlement work may continue; charging requires founder approval plus the first-dollar gate.

## 3. Initial go-to-market sequence

- **Issue:** Consumer demand and institutional distribution are both plausible and unproven.
- **Recommendation:** Run direct paired-family discovery and a narrow credit-union design-partner discovery track in parallel, without custom enterprise work or invented pipeline.
- **Alternatives:** Consumer-only, partner-first, caregiver-benefit, adviser, insurer, or telecom-led entry.
- **Tradeoff:** Consumer work yields faster learning but potentially expensive acquisition; partners offer trust and reach but introduce long cycles, diligence, and roadmap pressure.
- **Why founder / can work continue?:** This allocates scarce founder time and determines whose feedback shapes the company. Internal product work may continue; outreach or partner commitments require explicit authorization.

## 4. Human-assisted Safety Setup

- **Issue:** A staffed orientation may improve activation for lower-confidence users but adds liability, labor, scheduling, quality-control, and unit-economics risk.
- **Recommendation:** Defer the decision until moderated self-serve orientation tests establish the actual failure points; then pilot a tightly scripted, paid or partner-funded service if warranted.
- **Alternatives:** Self-serve only, founder-led concierge, outsourced specialist delivery, or partner-delivered setup.
- **Tradeoff:** Human help can create trust and learning but can turn a software business into an inconsistent service operation.
- **Why founder / can work continue?:** This changes the operating model and gross margin. Self-serve research may continue; no concierge promise or hiring should begin by default.

## 5. First external investment envelope

- **Issue:** The highest risks are evidence, safety, consent, and usability—not missing feature volume.
- **Recommendation:** Fund paired user research, an independently adjudicated fraud/action corpus, security/privacy review, and disabled older-adult accessibility testing before broad acquisition. Approve one intelligence pilot only against measured marginal lift.
- **Alternatives:** Remain founder-only longer, spend first on native capture/brand, or pursue growth and partnerships immediately.
- **Tradeoff:** Early assurance work is expensive and may invalidate the thesis, but growth spend before trust and retention evidence can amplify harm and waste.
- **Why founder / can work continue?:** Only the founder can set capital exposure and runway. Local work may continue; purchases, vendors, hiring, and external commitments need a defined budget and authorization.

## Not delegated to a founder decision

The local Run 1 PASS is not permission to accept unresolved production risk. Production identity/KMS, representative fraud evidence, legal/privacy review, accessibility evidence, dependency remediation, durable operations, commerce controls, and support/incident ownership remain evidence gates. A future launch decision may weigh residual risk only after those controls are demonstrated.
